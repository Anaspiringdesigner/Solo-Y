\section{Design Decisions}

\subsection{Why the interactions?}

\begin{displayquote}
\textcolor{Sepia}{"Aesthetic experiences can recruit neural systems involved in reward and valuation, so aesthetically pleasing stimuli are processed as rewarding or pleasurable in the brain."}. \sideparencite{Chatterjee2014Neuroaesthetics}.
\end{displayquote}

\begin{displayquote}
\textcolor{Sepia}{"This would suggest that participants with ADHD might be hypersensitive to reward because they experience receiving a reward relative to no reward as more pleasurable than control participants.”}. \sideparencite{vonRhein2015RewardADHD}.
\end{displayquote}

Combining these two research studies, An ADHD brain perceives aesthetically pleasing things as a stronger reward than its neurotypical cousin. \\

This meant that the output should have a very visually stimulating form. This is why Touch Designer has been choses to program visually stimulating interactions.  \\

During the event/trigger period, the user is trying to relax. Watching the HRV to relax would prove to be stressful. The visuals soften the stress of achieving a goal by gamifying it a little. \\

Instead of focusing on the number and stressing out, the user can look at the visual and try to control it with the intervention/action that they are going to perform during this 3-minute window. \\

Lastly, the visuals themselves act as a reward for someone with ADHD. \\

The data that the app streams is TS, HR, HRV, BR. TS stands for Time stamp. This data goes into the TCN encoder. What this encoder is doing is that it is trying to understand the stress patterns aginst time. So after a certain point of time, The RL will start predicting upcoming stress patterns and then it will pre-trigger an event asking the user to relax. \\

This encoder is what brings the real-time feedback down the line to the project. \\


Then the data is being sent to the RL for it to make a decision and understand the world policy. Decision is the action that RL agent takes. In this case the suggestion the RL model is giving to the user by selecting a visual and an action card is the action that the RL agent is taking. \\

Once an action has been taken, the relevant data will be sent to Touch designer via the touch design bridge. It is a specific module of the project that handles all communication with the touch designer.\\


\subsection{RL Action Space}
The RL action space consists of 2 different sets of things. One is the 5 touch designer interactions, and the other is the set of 8 dashboards - actions that the user can do during the 3-minute window. \\ 

So the RL action space will have a total of 5 x 8 = 40 discrete actions. So, when an event is triggered, the RL will pick one Interaction, and One Intervention to suggest the user. This combination of one visual + one action is one step of the RL model. The model takes a step every time an event is triggerd. \\

Out of the 8 dashboards, 5 of them are pre-loaded. Remaining 3 are user defined. Dashboards are pruned if not user even once for 15 consecutive steps. 15 Consecutive steps roughly comes around to 3-4 days in terms of real life. \\

RL personalization the personalization feature of the project. \\

\subsection{Data That comes to TD}

The TD receives the data, and it executes the data that was received and renders the visual. The visual is taken out of touch designer to OBS studio, which is then streamed by OBS studio to the mediamtx server on the device. This is done because the current touch designer license is free tier. If it were commercial tier, TD can directly stream the visual to the app. Tailscale is a vpn to ensure that the connection between the app and the server doesn't break when the app connects to a different network. \\

\begin{marginfigure}
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{images/tdinput.jpg}
  \caption{The input data that TD receives from the RL}
  \label{fig:margin-image}
\end{marginfigure}

Some of the notable data that comes to TD, of course apart from the vitals involves the following. 
\begin{description}
  \item[biofeedback/ping] It ensures that the TD is connected to the server and is running. 
  \item[biofeedback/hr] The heart rate
  \item[biofeedback/hrv] The heart rate variance
  \item[biofeedback/br] The breathing rate
  \item[biofeedback/interaction] This is the decision that the RL makes. Right now it is 5, showing the progress/growth visual. The visuals 0-4 are the ones in the RL action space. Visual 5 is not in the RL action space and is default before and after the 3-minute event widow. 
  \item[biofeedback/reward] This is the reward for the last interaction. 
  \item[biofeedback/trigger] This is the type of trigger. 1 is calendar, 2 is manual trigger showed in the demo, and 3 is internal stress based trigger. 
  \item[biofeedback/holding] This is either 1 or 0. It is 1 when an event is live.  
  \item[biofeedback/hold\_steps] Each 3 minute trigger is divided into 36 steps. 5 seconds per step. This is to ensure that the TD receives a data point every 5 seconds. \sidenote{The sensor logs the data as per its sampling rate. For example if sampling rate is 1hz, it logs 1 data line per second. we need all this data for encoding but we don't need it for the touch designer and the interaction itself. So only the latest data point is sent to TD every 5 seconds.}
  \item[biofeedback/hold\_progress] This is either 0 or 1. 1 if the hold is successful complete and 0 when it's live.
  \item[biofeedback/cumulative reward] This is the sum of all the rewards for all the actions taken so far. This is to show the progress/growth visual to keep the user engaged.    
\end{description}

\subsection{How is the HR, HRV, and BR data used?}

The data that is in the main area is sent into the individual subcomponents called base comps of TD. The chops in these base comps will receive the data and then, subsequent math chop and lag chop will normalize the data between a range, and it will be sent into the smoothing chop to reduce spikes. \\

That smooth hrv is then hooked up with various parameters in the Tops and pops that make up each visual.

  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{images/hrvuse.jpg}
  \caption{HRV processing inside each individual visual}
  \label{fig:margin-image}
\end{marginfigure}

The exact same process is applied for hr, br, and cumulative reward as well. 

\subsection{The Reward}

The reward is what enables the RL to learn the world policy, which is the user physiological state and their preferred actions that will improve their physiological state.\\

In English the reward for our RL model that I designed can be summarized as  \\

\begin{displayquote}
\textcolor{Sepia}{"Maximize the increase of HRV and reduce of HR in a 4:1 ratio"}. 
\end{displayquote}

The HRV has 4 times the weightage as heart rate. This is empirical for now. The idea is to calibrate this for each user. The weightage starts at 4:1 right now because of a general use of the 80-20 rule. \\

The current market heavily uses HRV as an indicator of stress and well-being. That's reason why HRV is a part of the reward function and is being used at a higher weightage. \\

As per\sideparencite{Rash2016ADHDCardiacVagalControl}, Heart rate has also been made part of the reward function. \\

The components of the reward function have been validated as per the above said reasons, but the ratio itself needs more data and advanced user testing and resarch to finalize. As stated earlier the ratio is meant to be a starting point and will be calibrated specific to the user with time and data. \\

\section{Why Hyper and Hypo Arousal?}

\begin{displayquote}
\textcolor{Sepia}{"According to state regulation theory, performance problems in ADHD arise partly because individuals have difficulty regulating activation, effort, and arousal in response to changing task demands, leading to under- or over-activation and more variable performance"} \sideparencite{vanDerMeere2005StateRegulationADHD}
\end{displayquote}

This theory hypothesizes that people with ADHD are in a constant flux of two states. Hyper Arousal and Hypo Arousal. This is the reason why the two states of hypo and hyper arousal were used in the design of the visuals. \\

\section{Why this solution?}

On a general higher level, what 0.618 is trying to do is, it is trying to inject little 3-minute packages of relaxation into the user's daily life. The reason for this is as follows \\

\begin{displayquote}
\textcolor{Sepia}{"Aja L Murray in her paper and her subsequent podcast with adhduk, states that stress and ADHD have a co-dependent relationship. A stressful event will worsen the ADHD symptoms down the line, and vice versa."} \sideparencite{Murray2025EmotionRegulationADHD}
\end{displayquote}

So we are trying to use this research to do the opposite. Inject positive events in the user's life to reduce the ADHD symptoms down the line. This is a positive reinforcement cycle. It feeds of itself. 0.618 is giving a platform to start this cycle. \\

\section{Validation Study}

To completely validate the project, we need to conduct a significant user study. The users should use the app for a couple of weeks to see the imapact of the app. The following are the ones that will be particularly investigated. \\
\begin{itemize}
  \item The relationship between the user positive reward and the user mental state after the 3-minute interaction. Does a positive reward (the number in the model) really correlate with a positive outcome for the user? It should as High HRV means positive stress-free state. We just need to confirm that in this study for our app.
  \item The assumptions made - the number of dashboards, the visuals, the interaction trigger (both calendar and stress based.)
\end{itemize}
